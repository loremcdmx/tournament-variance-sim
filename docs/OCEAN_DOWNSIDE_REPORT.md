# Ocean KO downside comparison

The second MTT tab compares the mechanical engine's downside paths. The first tab remains the full mechanical calculator. The report runs only on request, uses one cancellable worker and reports the configuration actually executed, including seed, number of careers and number of entries per career.

The primary form contains ticket, AFS, ROI and distance, with presets through 100,000 tournaments. Both AFS and distance accept up to 100,000. The UI uses the calculator's tournament terminology, counting each re-entry separately; empirical methodology still distinguishes that exposure from unique player–tournament events. Sample count, seed and Mystery spread remain in the accuracy disclosure. Results show their own scenario snapshot and warn when the draft changes. On narrow screens, depth and duration tables switch between below-peak and below-EV metrics. Recovery and model diagnostics remain available in disclosures.

The report prioritizes values and comparisons. Longer explanations, empirical evidence, jackpot mechanics and numerical precision are disclosures, while model status, censored durations and pointwise-envelope semantics stay beside the affected values. Results and inputs survive switching to the mechanical tab; leaving during calculation cancels the worker. Up to three complete four-format reports are reused in memory for an exact configuration match, never across reloads or releases. Reuse does not generate additional samples. Every row carries its sorted per-career maxima (16 KB per metric at 2,000 careers, 40 KB at 5,000), so the risk curves are built in the UI after the rows arrive and the cache needs nothing else.

For cold runs each format is compiled once. The report-specific collector skips unused chord scans and individual high-resolution paths; the ordinary simulation collector is unchanged. Regression checks compare every report value with the full collector across formats, fields, ROI and jackpot cases. All per-entry observations and up to 201 checkpoint values used by this report are preserved.

## What is compared

Freezeout, PKO, Mystery and Ocean KO use the same full ticket, field, target ROI, horizon and sample count. The controlled presets use an 8% fee; cash/bounty allocations and native payout shapes remain format-specific and are shown in the report. Equal ROI is a counterfactual comparison, not a claim that one player's skill earns equal ROI in every format. PKO uses head variance 0.4 and no heat; the Mystery log-variance parameter is configurable. That parameter (default 2) is an assumption, not calibrated to data: in the default scenario the Mystery σ runs from 4.9 to 11.5 BI for values 0–4, and the order of Ocean and Mystery by streak length can change with it. No leaderboard, rakeback, re-entry, tilt or changing skill is added.

The fixed comparison set is `freezeout`, `pko`, `mystery` and `ocean-ko`. Battle Royale remains available in the mechanical calculator but is neither simulated nor shown in this report. Space KO is shown separately through the existing published moment anchors; no empirical trajectories are invented from those moments.

## Path metrics

Let `P_t` be cumulative profit after entry `t`, `E_t` its analytic expectation and `H_t=max(0,P_1,...,P_t)` its running high. One BI is the full ticket including the fee.

- Maximum drawdown: `max_t(H_t-P_t)`.
- Maximum EV shortfall: `max_t(max(0,E_t-P_t))`.
- Longest below-EV streak: consecutive entry endpoints with `P_t<E_t`.
- Longest underwater streak: consecutive entry endpoints below the prior profit high.
- Longest losing-entry streak: consecutive entries with negative net profit, including bounty and the full cost.
- Fraction below EV: the number of endpoints with `P_t<E_t`, divided by the career length.
- Final loss: `P_N<0`. Final underperformance: `P_N<E_N`. A zero-profit career is not a loss.

The collector observes every entry of every simulated career. It does not depend on the visible trajectory subset or the checkpoint grid. Maxima and streak lengths are calculated per career before computing their median, P90, P95 or P99. Ties with EV or the previous high end their respective streaks. A tie is a difference within `1e-9` of a ticket, so float noise in a break-even entry (a Battle Royale entry can sum to `-1.78e-15`) is neither a losing entry nor a dip below the previous high.

Recovery reuses the engine's existing definition: from the trough of the deepest drawdown to the first **strictly higher** profit peak. Its duration summary is conditional on recovery within the selected horizon. The report separately shows the fraction whose deepest drawdown has not recovered by that horizon and the count with no drawdown. This is right censoring, not proof that a career will never recover. It is different from the longest underwater streak, which includes the descent and ends on equality with the old high.

## Uncertainty

Probability estimates include Wilson 95% intervals for Monte Carlo sampling uncertainty, conditional on the model. They do not include model error or uncertainty in a player's ROI. An observed zero count still has a positive upper bound. P99 and the largest observed drawdown are particularly unstable with a small sample; the maximum is not a worst-case guarantee.

The lower trajectory graph uses pointwise P05 profit minus EV at selected endpoints. P05 is not an actual career or a band containing 95% of entire careers. Exact path maxima are reported separately. Independent simulated entries with a fixed player model do not establish serial independence of real tournament results. All formats share one seed. With equal fields they draw the same random numbers, so careers of different formats are positively correlated (Spearman correlation of final profit 0.56–0.89 in the 8 October 2026 check). Monte Carlo and Wilson intervals describe each format on its own; they are not intervals for the differences between formats.

The aggregate best/worst comparison uses pointwise minimum and maximum profit across **all** simulated careers. Up to 201 actual engine checkpoints are retained, including both endpoints; no synthetic interpolation is used to create observations. All four formats share one plot and one BI scale: format is encoded by color, worst by a solid line, and best by a dashed line. Best/worst/EV and format toggles rescale to the visible curves; hiding best runs exposes downside detail without jackpot compression. The common dotted EV reference uses the completed scenario's target ROI times entries (zero in EV-deviation view). Profit/EV-deviation controls and a shared checkpoint selector expose the exact values. These envelopes combine different careers and become more extreme as the sample size grows; neither is an actual path or a guaranteed bound.

Threshold-risk curves count each career's exact maximum against 101 thresholds, `maximum >= threshold`; lines simply join observed probabilities. The grid is common to all formats in a chart and is built separately for drawdown and for EV shortfall: it runs from 0 to the first round number (1, 1.2, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4, 5, 6 or 8 times a power of ten) strictly above the deepest career of any shown format. Nothing is cut: on a 20,000-entry distance 12% of freezeout careers pass 1000 BI in drawdown and 34–37% in EV shortfall, which the former fixed 0–1000 BI grid could not show. Every curve ends at zero. The default display ends one real point after **every** shown format reaches 0.5% or less; if that never happens, it retains the whole grid. A full-tail checkbox, named with the actual top of the grid, restores it, and the data table always retains all 101 points and their Wilson intervals. This display cutoff does not remove outcomes, cap payouts or change probabilities.

Analytic standard deviation is supplementary. Ocean's lower/upper variance bounds are model bounds, not a fitted point estimate or confidence interval. Variance ratios describe final-profit variance or mean-estimation precision under independence; they are not ratios of drawdown depth or streak duration.

## Reading the headline card

The longest spell below EV is close to the whole distance for almost every career: a profit line tends to stay on one side of its EV for most of the path (the arcsine law). In the default scenario its P95 is 0.975–1.000 of the distance in every format at 1,000, 5,000 and 20,000 entries, so a longer horizon neither shortens nor separates it. Only the median tells the formats apart (at 1,000 entries Ocean 434, freezeout 371, Mystery 355, PKO 344). The card therefore shows the median of this spell, the tables print "≈ full distance" when the P95 is at least 97% of the distance (the number stays in a tooltip), and the advice that a longer horizon will show more applies only to the spell below the previous peak: for that spell the P95 is also about the whole distance at 1,000 entries (0.97–1.00 in all four formats, so the default table shows "≈ full distance" there too), but it stops saturating as the horizon grows: its share of the distance falls to about 0.7–0.96 at 5,000 and about 0.33–0.69 at 20,000 entries (freezeout highest, PKO lowest). In absolute entries the P95 still rises, but the spell no longer fills the distance, so a longer horizon does give more information about it.

The card also shows the ratios of Ocean's P95 to freezeout, PKO and Mystery, for drawdown and EV shortfall. They are rounded to one digit: the second digit is inside Monte Carlo noise (about ±0.02–0.04 at 2,000 careers). The ratios depend on the distance. In the default scenario the drawdown ratio Ocean / PKO is about 1.2, 1.4 and 1.4–1.5× at 1,000, 5,000 and 20,000 entries, because rare jackpots weigh more on long distances.

## Jackpots and empirical evidence

The wheel cutoff table replaces one ordinary Ocean wheel multiplier `M` by `min(M,cap)` without restoring the removed EV. It is not a capped-tournament ROI or drawdown simulation: applying a cutoff throughout the inherited bounty tree changes later head sizes and tier transitions. No full jackpot ROI attribution is claimed.

The public Space anchor caps total bounty per player/event while retaining cash and all paid entries. Its joint moments support exposure-adjusted means, variances and cash/bounty covariance. They contain no ordered tournament sequences, so they cannot validate the mechanical streak lengths. The Ocean moment transfer is a separate structural scenario and does not force the same ROI as the mechanical report. Its existing support limits and precision warnings remain visible. The Ocean card there is labelled as a scenario with capped bounties: its reference ROI is computed with the capped bounties and sits below the uncapped one, and its σ is the capped-moment σ. A one-line decomposition splits the σ gap between Ocean and Space into the part produced by the prize pool (regular prizes are 42% of the ticket in Ocean against 40% in Space, so mean prizes scale by their ratio) and the part produced by the bounties; it is computed from the transported result for the selected cap, ticket and scenario. See [the empirical model](OCEAN_EMPIRICAL_MODEL.md).
